Before this

Earlier generative models of images, such as Boltzmann machines, could in principle describe a whole picture, but drawing a sample from them meant running a slow chain of random adjustments, and training them was hard. Both of these papers asked for something simpler: a network that takes a handful of random numbers and returns a finished picture in one pass.

Earlier generative models of images, such as deep Boltzmann machines, defined a distribution over whole images, but sampling required long Markov chain Monte Carlo runs and training was difficult. Both papers instead train a generator network that maps a random latent vector to an image in a single forward pass.

Formally: learn a generator \(G_\theta\) such that \(G_\theta(z)\), with \(z\sim\mathcal{N}(0, I)\), is distributed like the data, so that sampling costs one forward pass.

In the walk

Images need a different walker: a painter, whose product is the canvas rather than the footprints. These first painters make the whole picture in a single stroke from a random seed. It is hardly a walk at all, just one step.

In the mechanism

Both turn a random vector into an image in one pass of a network. The VAE learns this alongside an encoder that maps images back to seeds. The GAN learns it against a critic that tries to tell painted pictures from real ones: Turing's judge, turned into a trainer.

\[x = G_\theta(z),\qquad z\sim\mathcal{N}(0, I)\]

One forward pass per sample. The VAE trains \(G_\theta\) as a decoder, alongside an encoder \(q_\phi(z\mid x)\), by maximizing a lower bound on \(\log p_\theta(x)\). The GAN trains it against a discriminator \(D\), with no likelihood at all.

A closer look: two ways to teach a one-stroke paintertwo training objectives for a one-pass generator

The VAE trains with a translator. An encoder looks at a real picture and returns not one seed but a small cloud of seeds, a center and a spread. The painter draws one seed from that cloud and must paint the original picture back. Training balances two pulls: paint the picture back accurately, and keep every cloud close to one shared, standard cloud. The second pull is what lets you skip the encoder later. Since every picture's cloud sits in the same region, any random seed from that region lands on ground the painter knows.

The GAN trains against a critic. No encoder at all. The painter turns random seeds into pictures; the critic sees a mix of painted and real pictures and guesses which is which. The painter is trained to fool the critic, and the critic to stop being fooled. The paper compares them to counterfeiters and police, each improving because of the other.

Both have characteristic failures, and both come from the single stroke:

  • Blur (VAE). When several pictures are plausible for one seed, a painter scored pixel by pixel does best by painting their average, which looks soft.
  • Mode collapse (GAN). A painter that finds a few pictures the critic accepts can keep painting only those. Training can also swing back and forth without settling.

VAE. An encoder maps an image \(x\) to a Gaussian over codes, \(q_\phi(z\mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \operatorname{diag}\sigma^2_\phi(x))\), and a decoder defines \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\). Training maximizes the evidence lower bound:

\[\log p_\theta(x) \ \ge\ \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0, I)\big)\]

The first term is reconstruction; the KL term keeps every posterior near the shared prior, so at generation time \(z\) can be drawn from \(\mathcal{N}(0, I)\) and decoded with no encoder. The reparameterization \(z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x)\odot\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), makes the sampling step differentiable.

GAN. No encoder and no explicit likelihood. A generator \(G\) and a discriminator \(D\) play a two-player minimax game:

\[\min_G \max_D\ \ \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\text{data}}}\big[\log D(x)\big] + \mathbb{E}_{z\sim p(z)}\big[\log\big(1 - D(G(z))\big)\big]\]

For the optimal \(D\), the generator's objective equals \(-\log 4 + 2\,\mathrm{JSD}(p_{\text{data}}\,\|\,p_G)\), minimized exactly when \(p_G = p_{\text{data}}\). The paper compares the game to counterfeiters and police.

Each has a characteristic failure, and both follow from generating in one pass:

  • Blur (VAE). With a factorized Gaussian likelihood, \(-\log p_\theta(x\mid z)\) is squared error, and for a code compatible with several images the optimal decoder output is their mean, \(\mathbb{E}[x\mid z]\), which looks soft.
  • Mode collapse and instability (GAN). \(G\) can cover only a few modes of \(p_{\text{data}}\) that fool \(D\), and simultaneous gradient steps on a minimax objective can oscillate instead of converging.

What it changed

GANs dominated image generation for years and reached photographic faces by 2018 and 2019. They were overtaken by the painters of the 2020 entry, which are slower but steadier to train and cover more variety.

The VAE's idea lasted in a different role. Many diffusion systems, Stable Diffusion among them, paint not on pixels but on a VAE's compressed seed-ground, and use the VAE's decoder to turn the result into an image at the end. The GAN's critic survives as the clearest picture of training by a judge.

GANs led image generation for several years, reaching photorealistic faces with StyleGAN (2018–19). Diffusion models overtook them on sample diversity and training stability.

The VAE persisted as a component. Latent diffusion models, Stable Diffusion among them, run diffusion in a VAE's latent space and decode the result to pixels. The GAN's discriminator remains the canonical example of a learned critic used as a training signal.

Left open

One stroke can't correct itself. VAE pictures came out blurry; GANs were unstable to train and prone to painting the same few pictures. The painter needed drafts.\(x = G_\theta(z)\) is computed in a single pass, so its errors can't be corrected: VAE samples were blurry, and GANs were unstable and prone to mode collapse.
