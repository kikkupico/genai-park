Trails

Terrain and trails: a compressed picture of statistical AI

A search for one compressed representation of decades of statistical AI research. The aim is to see the field's results as variations on one picture, not to teach LLMs or any single technique.

Started 2026-11-20 Sister project: DAAG2
Current thesis An AI system's product is its own walk. Terrain decides what the walker can tell apart, trails record where walks went before, and scouting looks ahead.
Vocabulary

Five words

Walk
The product. One traversal, one irreversible local step at a time: the tokens generated, the moves played.
Blaze
The mark read at a junction: the distribution over next steps. The walker sees only the blaze in front of it and the road behind.
Trails
Where past walks went, and where they ended well. Worn in by many walkers: counts, likelihood, reward.
Terrain
What the walker can tell apart, and what counts as nearby. Representation, architecture, inductive bias. It decides where a trail could run at all.
Scouting
Walks taken only to choose the real one, then discarded. Search, lookahead, rollouts.

Why trails and not signposts: a signpost is put up by someone, and a trail is worn in by many walkers. That is the difference between symbolic AI, which posted its signs by hand, and statistical AI, which wears them in from data.

The criterion

The trace is the product

Every algorithm is a sequence of steps, so "it is a walk" would say nothing on its own. What separates AI from ordinary computation is what happens to the steps.

Computation

The trace is scaffolding. Dijkstra outputs a path, but the nodes it expanded along the way are thrown away. A mistake midway costs nothing if the answer comes out right.

AI

The trace is delivered. Each token is read, each move is played, each action lands in the world. A mistake midway is a defect in the product, and it stays.

This is why the walk is literally true. A language model is a distribution over paths, and the chain rule factors any such distribution into one local blaze per junction. A policy does the same for trajectories. It also explains why the field's chronic problems are path problems: exposure bias, compounding error, hallucination, and the lack of a way back.

Pen and brush

The walk builds the product as it goes. Some walkers draw with a pen: the line of ink is the text, so the footprints are the product. That is an autoregressive model. Others paint with a brush on a canvas they carry: the strokes are lost and the painting remains. That is diffusion, which blocks in the composition first and the detail last, the way a painter does. Either way, the walker looks at the work so far before each step.

Pen and brush differ in the order they build the product, not in how they move through the possibilities. Both make one walk through the tree of possible outputs and touch one junction per level. The difference shows on a second tree, made of the product's own parts. The pen finishes each part before starting the next. The brush refines every part a little on each pass, coarse to fine, which visits the product's levels of detail one level at a time.

This gives the criterion a test: is every intermediate state a rough draft of the product? A text prefix is a partial text, and a half-denoised canvas is a blurry version of the final image, so both are walks. Dijkstra's distance table is no draft of a path, because the path is pulled out at the end, so it stays computation. A painter can paint over earlier work, so for the brush "a mistake midway stays" softens to "early strokes mostly stay." And because two stroke orders can give the same canvas, a brush walker's paths merge: it crosses a terrain, not a tree.

A walker can be given two things at the start: road already laid, and a destination named in advance. A user's prompt is road, which the walker finishes. An inpainting mask or an image-to-image draft is the brush version, and image-to-image starts the walk partway along. A text-to-image prompt is never painted; the painter keeps it beside the canvas and glances at it before every stroke, and guidance sets how strictly. Chat models write both kinds into the road. A system prompt is road too, but it is posted once and read by every walker, so it does the job of a signpost.

The Turing test judges footprints

Turing's test (1950) looks only at the footprints and asks whether a person made them. It never asks where the walk ended. That is the criterion above in its oldest form: the trace is what gets judged.

It is also the judge most of the field trains against. Maximum likelihood wears trails so that the walker's footprints match human ones, step by step. Trails are footprints that have piled up, and the test reads one set of them.

Its blind spot is the field's blind spot. Footprints can look human and still lead nowhere, and a hallucination is human-looking footprints to a place that does not exist. The two judges in M1 split along this line: imitation judges the footprints, and reward judges where they ended.

The picture

Four roles

The walker sees only the blaze in front of it and the road behind. There is no map. Its position is the whole road behind it, so the space is a tree and no junction is visited twice. The terrain decides which junctions look alike, so a blaze can be guessed at a junction no walker has passed.

RoleWhat survivesExamples
WalkingThe path. It is the product.Decoding, acting, generating, a policy running
ScoutingOnly the choice. Scouting walks are discarded.Beam search, Viterbi, MCTS rollouts, tree of thoughts, lookahead, a speculative draft
Wearing trailsThe trails: which steps are likely, which routes ended well.Counting n-grams, maximum likelihood, TD learning, policy gradients, RLHF
Building terrainWhat the walker can tell apart.Hand-made features, kernels, embeddings, architectures, representation learning

Scouting, wearing trails and building terrain are ordinary computation by the criterion above: their own traces are scaffolding. They matter because they serve the one walk that is delivered.

Two things, one or two processes

Terrain and trails

Terrain and trails are different things. Whether they are built by different processes has changed over time, and that change is one of the field's main storylines.

EraTerrain built byTrails worn by

The 2012 fusion is the point where the terrain stopped being built by hand.

Garden or forest, signposts or trails

Terrain can be laid out like a manicured garden, with every bed planted by design. It can also be grown like a forest from scattered seed. Trails can be posted or worn. The two choices are independent, so there are four corners, and the field has filled all of them.

TerrainSignposts posted by handTrails worn in
GardenSymbolic AI: expert systems, A* with hand-made heuristicsStatistical learning before 2012: hand-made features, kernels, HMMs with hand-chosen states
ForestSystem prompts: road posted once by hand and read by every walker who enters the grown forestDeep learning from 2012

So the garden ends in 2012, not where symbolic AI ends. Statistical AI kept gardening its terrain for decades while letting the trails wear in. The seeds are not thrown blindly, either. The architecture decides what can grow, which is why architecture counts as terrain.

A prediction the frame makes

Trails worn only by your own walks narrow and fade. That is model collapse, seen when models train on their own outputs (Shumailov et al. 2024). Self-play avoids it because a destination check, a win or a loss, keeps correcting the trails. The frame was not built to explain this, which is a good sign.

Q1 · the test, run 2026-11-20

Fifty-one results, tagged

Results were picked for their weight in the field, not for how well they fit. Likely misfits were included on purpose. Each result gets one primary role, the move it made, the failure it answered, and a fit grade.

  • Native: the contribution can be stated in the five words without losing its main point.
  • Stretched: it can be stated, but a word is bent or the main point is lost.
  • Outside: it needs words the frame does not have.

Rule for training methods: a training method is tagged by what it changes in the finished walker. If it only reaches the same walker more cheaply or reliably, it is machinery and sits outside the frame, because training's trace is scaffolding.

Verdict

The frame holds, with one amendment. 37 of 51 results are native, and as predicted the misfits cluster in terrain and in machinery. The amendment: the walk builds the product as it goes, with a pen or with a brush (see the criterion). Before it, VAE and diffusion formed a third cluster of misfits.

Where the 14 misfits fall

  • Terrain theory (5): SVMs, boosting, LDA, BERT, EM. Six of the 18 terrain rows are misfits, against four of the 22 trail rows.
  • Machinery (6): deep belief nets, ResNet, optimizers and normalization, scaling laws, GPT-2 and speculative decoding. Their point is that training works, how well it scales, or how fast the same walk runs. The frame has no word for cost.
  • Adversarial game (1): GAN. Its generator is a one-stroke painter, which fits, but the frame has no word for the game between generator and judge.
  • Messages (1): belief propagation, which belongs to the DAAG2 frame.
  • Whole-path scoring (1): CRFs have no local blaze. They score whole paths and are decoded by scouting (Viterbi). This marks a real edge: the blaze is locally normalized by definition.

Three findings the frame did not set out to make

  • Few results change the walk itself, at least in this selection. Only two rows have walking as their role: nucleus sampling, and diffusion, which brought the brush. Against them stand 22 for trails, 18 for terrain and 6 for scouting. The selection left out beam search, temperature and top-k, so this may say more about the pick than about the field. It is a claim to test alongside Q5: if the walker is the decoding rule, decoding results are exactly the ones that change the walker.
  • "Recovery from wrong turns" is a trail source. DAgger, scheduled sampling, Samuel, TD-Gammon, AlphaZero and the reasoning models all wear trails at the junctions the walker itself reaches. What separates them is the judge: an expert, a win or loss, or a checkable answer. Model collapse is the same move with no judge at all. No result lets the walker step back. Recovery comes only through trails or outside scouting.
  • Viterbi is scouting, not walking. Its table is scaffolding and only the best path survives. The role table above has been corrected.

Stress tests

Boundary cases

Chain of thought

A walk demoted to scouting. The reasoning tokens stop being the product and only the final answer counts. Hidden reasoning makes the demotion visible. Part of a path-type system is turned back into a destination-type one.

Reasoning models "backing up"

They cannot walk back. They keep walking down a road that says "that was wrong." Real backtracking needs outside scouting, as in tree of thoughts.

Off the trail

Exposure bias. Trails are worn by routes people took, so once a walk leaves them the blazes are guesses from the terrain alone, and errors compound.

Diffusion sampling

A brush walk. The strokes are lost but the canvas is built along the way, and every intermediate canvas is a draft of the image. Diffusion language models are painters who fill in words in any order. Exposure bias carries over: a bad early wash spoils everything painted on top of it.

MCMC

Mixed. The samples along the chain are the result, but burn-in is thrown away.

Classification

The degenerate walk of one step. It fits, but the frame adds little; the interest lies in the terrain and in how the trail was worn.

A* and Dijkstra

They output a path, but their own trace is discarded. They are computation, and in this picture they are scouting tools.

Tools, retrieval, chat

The road is laid by more than one hand. A user's message, a tool's result and a retrieved passage are steps added to the road behind, and the walker carries on from the new position. Since its position is the whole road behind, nothing else changes. The walker still has no map: retrieved text is road, not map. RL's world, choosing the next junction after each action, is the same pattern. (From the user.)

Scope

What lives outside the walk

  • The terrain's own theory. SVMs and kernels, PCA, clustering, embeddings, architectures, GANs and learning theory are destination-type. They explain how terrain is built and why a trail can be trusted at junctions nobody has passed. They are told as terrain, without being forced into travel.
  • Message passing. Belief propagation, factor graphs and message-passing networks have no walker, only local messages between nodes. That is the DAAG2 frame, so these results may belong to the sister project.
  • The thesis is a stance. Much of 1990s and 2000s statistical AI produced answers, not paths. The picture reads the history as a movement from answer-producers to path-producers: classifiers, then generators, then agents. The site should say so openly.
