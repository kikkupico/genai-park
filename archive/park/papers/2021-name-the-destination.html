Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance (2021)

Before this

A diffusion painter can be shown a label or a caption during training, so it learns pictures that go with each. But in practice, conditioned painters often drifted from the caption. An earlier fix, classifier guidance, trained a separate image classifier that worked on noisy pictures and used it to push each draft toward the requested class. That meant training and running a second network.

Conditional diffusion models, trained with a class label or caption, often produced samples that matched the conditioning only loosely. Classifier guidance (Dhariwal and Nichol, 2021) improved this using the gradient of a separate classifier trained on noisy images, which meant training and running a second network.

Formally, classifier guidance adds \(s\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p_\phi(c\mid x_t)\), the gradient of a separately trained classifier \(p_\phi\), to each denoising step.

In the walk

A text-to-image painter is handed a destination: a card saying what the picture should be. The card is never painted. The painter keeps it beside the canvas and glances at it before every stroke. Guidance sets how hard it looks: closer to the card, at the cost of variety.

In the mechanism

One network is trained both with and without the text prompt. At each denoising step the two predictions are compared, and the difference is amplified by the guidance scale. This replaced an earlier method that needed a separate image classifier.

\[\tilde\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, c) = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\,\big(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big)\]

One network, trained with \(c\) sometimes replaced by a null token \(\varnothing\), gives both predictions. With \(w > 1\) the direction the conditioning adds is amplified, with no classifier at all.

A closer look: two glances and the difference between themcombining conditional and unconditional predictions

During training, the caption is blanked out a small fraction of the time. The same network therefore learns two skills: denoising toward a picture of this caption, and denoising toward any picture at all.

At each step of painting, it makes both predictions and compares them. The difference between them is the direction the card points: what this caption adds compared with a picture of anything. Guidance stretches that difference:

step = without card + w × (with card − without card)

At w = 1 this is just the ordinary conditioned painter. Larger values push each draft harder toward the card. Popular open text-to-image tools default to around 7.5. Too high and pictures become over-saturated and repetitive: closer to the card, less variety. The figure's two painters are two values of w.

The paper's experiments used class labels on ImageNet. Applied to text captions, the method was quickly adopted: GLIDE (2021) found that human raters preferred it to guiding with a separate image–text model.

During training, the conditioning \(c\) is replaced with a null token \(\varnothing\) for a small fraction of examples, so a single network learns both the conditional prediction \(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, c)\) and the unconditional \(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\).

At each sampling step, both are computed and combined:

\[\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\,\big(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big)\]

\(w = 1\) recovers the ordinary conditional model; \(w > 1\) extrapolates further in the direction the conditioning adds. (The paper writes the same rule as \((1+w')\,\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,c) - w'\,\varepsilon_\theta(x_t)\), so \(w = 1 + w'\).) Since \(\varepsilon_\theta \approx -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t)\), the difference term is proportional to \(-\nabla_{x_t}\log p(c\mid x_t)\), an implicit classifier, and guidance samples approximately from

\[\tilde p(x_t\mid c) \ \propto\ p(x_t)\,p(c\mid x_t)^{w}.\]

Popular open text-to-image tools default to about \(w = 7.5\). Higher \(w\) increases prompt adherence and sample fidelity while reducing diversity, and very high values cause oversaturated, repetitive images.

The paper's experiments used class-conditional ImageNet. GLIDE (2021) applied it to text conditioning and found human evaluators preferred it to CLIP guidance.

What it changed

It became the standard control in text-to-image and text-to-video systems, usually exposed as a "guidance scale" setting. In walker terms, the card stays beside the canvas the whole time, read by a text encoder and glanced at before every stroke. Nothing in the card is ever painted directly.

Card or road, it is all handed over at the start. Neither tells the painter how its finished pictures were received.

It became the standard conditioning method in text-to-image and text-to-video diffusion, usually exposed as the "guidance scale" parameter, with the prompt embedded by a text encoder and supplied at every denoising step.

Prompt or text context, the conditioning is fixed before generation. Neither provides feedback on how good the output was.
